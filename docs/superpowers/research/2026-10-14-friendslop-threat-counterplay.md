# Friend-slop threat counter-play - research for the day-time mafia patrol (M4)

_2026-10-14. Question from the user: if a mafia patrol interrupts fishing during the day, what real, fun way do players get to deal with it (beyond slowly driving away or hiding)? Method: our own notes (`2026-10-11-*-mechanics.md`, the fun formula, M4 design inputs) plus public web sources (wikis, guides, reviews). No reverse engineering, no downloads. Wiki and guide numbers disagree between patches, so treat every number as approximate; mechanics are what matters._

## 1. Per game: threats, tools, and why it is funny

### REPO
- **Threats:** roaming monsters that hunt by sound or sight. Huntsman is blind but hears everything; Trudge is slow, tanky, loud; Shadow Child only hurts if you stare at it; Gnomes come in groups and wreck loot; Robe/Clown punish line of sight; the "Duck" is a bait-and-switch trap.
- **Tools and counters:** hide and crouch (voice loudness is detected, so shouting gets you hunted: our note section 5), throw an item to pull a blind monster away, **stun items** (Stun Grenade: no damage, blinds and stuns about 15 s, about $3K; Stun Mine: stuns whatever steps on it; Shockwave Grenade adds damage), melee (frying pan, bat, sledgehammer; stamina-limited, recharged at the truck), a rotating-stock gun that is a rare luxury, and **grab-and-throw physics objects** (a thrown Gnome stuns other enemies). Guides tell crews to assign a teammate to guard the cart against loot-wrecking monsters.
- **Why it is funny, not punishing:** counters are cheap, non-lethal and improvised (throw anything); the loud/clumsy player causes the incident; you can cancel a monster by luring it with junk. Breaking things also makes noise, so greed and panic feed each other.
- Sources: https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-use-stun-grenade-repo , https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/repo-all-monsters-and-best-strategies-against-them , https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/all-monsters-repo , https://dotesports.com/indies/news/all-guns-in-r-e-p-o

### Lethal Company
- **Threats:** monsters in the facility (Bracken, Thumper, ...), turrets and landmines, and a hard quota deadline.
- **Tools and counters:** shovel (melee, about 3-5 hits for the common monsters), **Stun Grenade** (about 40 credits, several seconds of stun with a short fuse; some monsters immune), **Zap Gun** (immobilises while someone holds the arc, so one player zaps and another hits), flashlights, and the **ship terminal**: one friend at the ship watches the map and radar, opens security doors, and temporarily disarms turrets and landmines for the crew inside. Landmines can be set off safely by throwing a stun grenade or any throwable at them; hitting a mine with a melee weapon explodes it.
- **Why it is funny:** the support-from-the-ship role (one player is "the voice in your ear"), combos that need two people (zap + shovel), and the environment itself (mines, turrets) as the weapon. Mistakes kill friends in dumb ways.
- Sources: https://lethal.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun_grenade , https://lethal.miraheze.org/wiki/Landmine , https://lethal.miraheze.org/wiki/Tools_Guide , https://primagames.com/tips/how-to-use-all-items-in-lethal-company , https://commonsensegamer.com/lethal-company-all-equipment-guide/

### Content Warning
- **Threats:** randomised monsters in the "Old World"; each has a readable look, attack and sound.
- **Tools and counters:** stun tools are the only real "weapons" (Shock Stick, Goo Ball; no real damage, they buy time to escape), flashlights (some monsters dislike light), a defibrillator to revive a body, a healing item. 3 inventory slots. The camera turns a death or a revive into the objective (views).
- **Why it is funny:** failure is the content; the game rewards filming the disaster, so getting caught is not a loss of the fun, only of safety.
- Sources: https://primagames.com/gaming/all-items-in-content-warning-game-explained , https://ggrecon.com/guides/content-warning-all-items , https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/content-warning-how-to-revive-other-players-2629008/

### PEAK
- **Threats:** environmental, almost no combat: bees, scorpions, spiders, ticks, jellyfish, fog, weather per biome, falls.
- **Tools and counters:** **pick up and throw the scorpion**; throw objects to stun spiders then pass under; one player kites the bees while another loots the hive (bees chase only a short distance); teammates remove ticks, pull friends up, share food/medicine. Hazards have a cure item or a trick (our REPO+PEAK note sections 6 and 8).
- **Why it is funny:** hazards are small, readable and escapable; the funny part is friends mishandling them (throwing a scorpion the wrong way). The ragdoll "wobble" is a feature.
- Sources: https://peak.wiki.gg/wiki/Hazards , https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=317656 , https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=270378

### RV There Yet?
- **Threats:** bears, snakes, eagles, and vehicle terrain. No cops or police found in any source.
- **Tools and counters:** **bear spray**, a hammer (a miss hits you), crossing a bridge (bears will not), antidote for snakes, throwing items at snakes, a winch attached to the RV to catch someone an eagle grabs, an EpiPen to revive. Most animals cannot be killed; the game is avoidance, deterrence and a stocked RV shelf.
- **Why it is funny:** the counter is a consumable you remembered (or forgot) to bring; the RV itself is the safe base and the toolbox.
- Sources: https://www.destructoid.com/all-animals-in-rv-there-yet-and-how-to-defeat-them/ , https://www.destructoid.com/how-to-escape-from-bears-in-rv-there-yet/ , https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-get-away-eagles-rv-there-yet ; our note `2026-10-11-rv-there-yet-mechanics.md` section 4 (shark, piranha, snake: hazards have a cure item).

### Gamble With Your Friends (our own notes, no web needed)
- No monsters; the "threats" are friends and bad luck. Items are save-or-ruin: Bat (knockback), QuotaGun (shoot a friend), thrown Drink (buff a friend), Taser, refund and triple-or-nothing reels (65% odds), bad-beat bonuses, capped stakes so one bet never ends the run. See `2026-10-11-gamble-with-your-friends-mechanics.md` sections 3 and 6.

### Boat and ship co-op games (counter-play for a boat under pressure)
- **Sea of Thieves:** hull holes patched with planks from storage, water bailed with buckets, a flooded ship sinks; cannons, chain shot (breaks masts and wheel), boarding. Roles are conventions: helm, gunner, repair/bailer, boarder. World events (Kraken, Megalodon) cannot attack a ship next to an island, so cover is a tactic. https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/Naval_combat , https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/Kraken
- **Barotrauma:** crew roles (captain, engineer, mechanic, security, medic); big guns need a crew member alive at the station, monsters board through breaches; luring monsters onto an enemy is an accepted tactic. https://barotraumagame.com/wiki/Moping_Jack , https://earlyguides.com/barotrauma/classes
- **Deep Rock Galactic:** class toolkits (turrets, platform gun, flares), swarm waves with an extraction pod; friendly fire is on and part of the chaos. https://playstation.com/en-tw/games/deep-rock-galactic

## 2. Cross-game patterns that work for 1-4 friends

1. **Physics items as weapons.** REPO (thrown Gnomes, thrown items for lures), PEAK (thrown scorpions and objects), RV (thrown items at snakes), Lethal Company (stun-grenade-on-mine). The best weapon is whatever is near. FishGame already has fish and junk with mass.
2. **Cheap stuns over kills.** REPO Stun Grenade/Mine, Lethal Zap Gun, Content Warning Shock Stick/Goo Ball: no damage, a few seconds of opening. No game here asks 1-4 friends to defeat a threat by damage race.
3. **Spend loot to survive.** Bear spray, stun grenades, revive items all cost run money; REPO monsters wreck the loot itself. The cost is what keeps it tense: "do we burn the good fish to save the day?"
4. **Environmental tricks.** Mines and turrets turned against monsters (Lethal), bridges bears will not cross (RV), islands blocking the Kraken (Sea of Thieves), cauldrons and toilets killing the Duck (REPO). Map knowledge is a weapon.
5. **Two-person combos and role splits.** Zap + shovel, kite the bees + loot the hive, one at the terminal + three inside, helm + gunner + bailer. 4 seats on the tug fit this exactly.
6. **Separation risk and a safe base.** Proximity voice makes splitting up funny and scary; the truck, ship or RV is the base, tool shelf and revive point.
7. **Noise and sight are the dials.** REPO detects voice loudness and breaking sounds; Content Warning monsters have readable sounds and sights. Telegraphing is how a threat stays funny: you see it coming and still botch it.
8. **Comedy from failure.** Fail states are cheap, ragdoll-flavoured and spectator-friendly (hurled head, dropped by an eagle, missed hammer swing). Games reward the clip, not the clean win.

## 3. Counter-play options for the mafia day patrol

Shared frame for all options: a patrol boat (one boat, 2-3 goons) appears after a **tell phase**, closes in, then either "inspects" (boards, confiscates) or gives up. The cost of failure is **loot, not life**: goons confiscate fish in any ice chest left on deck or in the tug storage, and a chest not aboard is already at risk by design. Goons are non-lethal and dumb (dumb dialogue, side-eyeing, per the fun formula). Host-authoritative AI boat + a "patience / suspicion" value; counters push it up or down.

**Counter-tells the mafia gives (all options):** gulls scatter and the radio crackles (starfish god warns); a dark silhouette on the horizon 60-90 s ahead (the "wanted poster" atmosphere pays off here); engine hum rising in the mix; searchlight sweep and a horn on closing in; goons shout when they spot something suspicious. Stage 1 to 7 escalation: more goons, faster boat, a second patrol, a spotlight tug.

### A. Junk decoy: "throw the boot" (REPO lure + junk items)
- **How it plays:** toss any junk (old boot, can, tire, ...) or a stinky fish into the water or onto a drifting buoy; the patrol investigates the noise like REPO's blind monsters, wasting time and gaining "patience loss". A junk piece lures a patrol for N seconds; a pile of junk on a raft makes a bigger decoy. The patrol reacts with comic dialogue ("is that a boot?").
- **Reuses:** 7 junk items, fish physics/throw (planned M4/M5), noise system.
- **Cost / trade-off:** you lose the item (junk is cheap, so the cost is mild); a lucky/odd junk item could instead wake the patrol up (save-or-ruin).
- **Weight:** S-M.

### B. Hook the goon: "reel in the patrol" (fishing as the weapon)
- **How it plays:** cast at the patrol boat; hooking a goon, hat, searchlight or the boat's anchor line starts the normal fight (existing tension model). Win: yank the goon overboard (comic splash, ragdoll swim, patrol patience drops); lose: your line snaps and you are noticed. Two players can team up (one reels, one steers), the tug acts as an anchor.
- **Reuses:** fishing fight and tension model (R72), rods and bait (vendor), the existing hooked-target code, tug seats.
- **Cost / trade-off:** the cast is loud and marks you; a hard fight is a risk for the one fisher while others deal with the second goon.
- **Weight:** M-L (new hookable AI target and fight tuning). High comedy and uniquely FishGame.

### C. Fish as ammunition / quirk fish (physics + the quirk plan)
- **How it plays:** pull a fish from an ice chest and throw it. A normal fish slaps and stuns a goon for a couple of seconds (cheap stun); quirk fish act as grenades: pufferfish inflates on the deck, electric eel shocks the boat, a flopping fish tangles a goon. The more valuable the fish, the better the effect. Heavier fish hit harder (REPO mass-compensated throws).
- **Reuses:** ice chests (per-player), the carry/throw physics planned for M4, the 1-in-3 fish quirk plan from the fun formula.
- **Cost / trade-off:** **you throw away the day's income** (spend loot to survive); the fun is picking which fish to burn.
- **Weight:** M (throw stun on goons, quirk fish later).

### D. Toll and bribe: "pay the nice men" (spend loot to survive)
- **How it plays:** the patrol arrives and demands a toll. A player carries a chest to the rail (or throws fish onto the patrol boat) and the goons leave, cheaper if you have a good chest and a smooth talker (bluff mini-moment: whoever speaks into voice chat), or they raise the price if you stall. Haggle by dropping one fish at a time while goons count.
- **Reuses:** ice chests, tug storage, the selling pad logic (physical basket with a live total, from GWYF).
- **Cost / trade-off:** a guaranteed but expensive way out; the safe option every crew will fall back on, which keeps the harder options optional.
- **Weight:** S (re-use sell pad and a patrol "leave" state).

### E. Tug gadgets from the starfish god (items that save or ruin)
- **How it plays:** night-camp vendor sells deck gadgets crewed from the 4 seats: **bilge hose** (soaks goons, knocks them back), **ink/smoke barrel** (blocks the searchlight, patrol loses you), **oil slick** (patrol boat spins out), **air horn** (scares gulls and goons, but is also loud), **net cannon** (stuns a goon, uses a fish as shot). One item per night, limited stock; some are scams (the "blessed" flare works 65% of the time, backfires 35%, GWYF Angel's Reel pattern).
- **Reuses:** vendor (rods, bait, supplies), tug seats and interior storage, the stun and decoy states above.
- **Cost / trade-off:** shop money; a gadget is a consumable with a risk; a missing gunner means no shot.
- **Weight:** M (one gadget) to L (several, each with a station and animation).

### F. Environmental lure: map hazards as weapons (Lethal mines, SoT islands)
- **How it plays:** each map has terrain that fits its theme: shallows and snags on the creek, a dock rope at the lake, a mill wheel on the rapids, stilt shack debris in the swamp, falls and rocks at the canyon, hot springs at the volcano. Drive so the patrol cuts through it (they follow the tug), or ring a buoy bell to pull them onto a hazard. Hiding behind an island line makes the patrol lose sight (Sea of Thieves "cover").
- **Reuses:** map layout, tug driving (free driving), the stage list; no extra system.
- **Cost / trade-off:** needs a skilled driver and map knowledge; fishing time is lost while steering. Rewards replay.
- **Weight:** S per map once an AI boat avoids/ignores hazards (M for the AI pathing).

### G. Go dark: stealth and noise (REPO voice, baseline)
- **How it plays:** kill the engine, douse lights, cover the tug with a tarp, keep quiet. Noise (reeling, splashes, talking via voice loudness, engine) fills a suspicion meter; the patrol slows to a search pattern. Players who stay still are overlooked; loud ones draw the boat.
- **Reuses:** voice loudness idea from REPO, tug interior, the "hide" the user already has.
- **Cost / trade-off:** lost fishing time; bad luck with a noisy friend turns hiding into the joke. This is the safe baseline layer under all the other options.
- **Weight:** S-M.

### H. Shove and board defence (later)
- **How it plays:** goons board; players shove them off with oars and rods, goons are ragdolls (REPO/PEAK shove, Barotrauma boarders).
- **Reuses:** M5 shove, ragdoll, carry. Cost: only after those exist.
- **Weight:** L. **Park for M5.**

## 4. Recommended first version (M4) and what to leave

**Principles:** no kills, no fail states that end the run, tells before every beat, and one fast, cheap way out so the harder options stay fun rather than mandatory.

**M4 first version**
1. **The patrol loop:** silhouette tell (60-90 s) -> closing in (engine, searchlight, horn) -> inspection. A patience / suspicion value on the patrol with three outcomes: leaves (patience gone), moves on (you hid well), or boards and confiscates unprotected chests and tug-storage fish (suspicion full). Flat penalty, no run loss.
2. **G (go dark)** as the baseline, using noise as the dial.
3. **A (junk decoy)** as the cheapest active counter (reuses the 7 junk items, mostly a noise lure plus dialogue).
4. **C (fish/junk throw stun, simple form)** only the plain "slap a goon" version, no quirks yet.
5. **D (toll)** as the guaranteed safe exit (cost: fish).
6. **F (one environmental trick)** on the Lake Dock and Harbour Pier maps as a proof of concept.

This set reuses the junk, ice chests, tug storage, throw physics and free driving that M4 already builds, and needs one new AI boat and one patience value. It covers the user's "beyond hiding or slowly driving away" ask with three active tools (decoy, throw, driving trick) plus one paid exit.

**Leave for later**
- **B (hook the goon)**: the best fit for fishing but needs a new hookable target and fight tuning; try as an M4 stretch or the first M4.5 item.
- **E (tug gadgets and the blessed flare)** with the starfish god's stock: M4 late / M5 once the vendor exists; start with one gadget (ink barrel or hose) as proof.
- **C quirk fish, H (boarding and shove)**: after M3 quirk data and M5 ragdolls.
- Stage escalation (bigger patrols, a second boat, a spotlight tug) and the finale joke (mafia dying in a dumb way) stay in the story plan, not this system.

**Playtest questions for the user:** does the toll make the other options feel optional? Is a boarding that only costs fish tense enough? Which of B or C feels more "fishing"?
